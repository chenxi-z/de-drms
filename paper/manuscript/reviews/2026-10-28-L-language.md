## Findings that answer “还缺什么”

1. **C — The scientific contribution still lacks a completed comparison with its closest synthesis antecedents.** `sections/00-related-work.tex:15` (ZH `sections-zh/00-related-work.tex:15`) explicitly ends with “Draft note: a proposition-level comparison with the four synthesis papers remains to be completed.” The preceding sentences identify the synthesis context but do not tell the reader which recovery/uniqueness result is inherited, specialized, or new. The introduction does state an objective (`01-introduction.tex:9`); it is not missing. What is missing is the evidence-based boundary of the contribution relative to the four named works. This is a paragraph-function problem under L1/L4/L7 and §2b Introduction, not a complaint about an unattractive phrase. Complete that comparison and use its conclusion to state the specific unresolved problem; simply deleting the draft note does not provide the missing positioning. No claim of plagiarism or false novelty is made.

2. **C — Reported relative differences are not operationally defined.** `sections/04-simulation-results-and-output-coupling.tex:9` (ZH same file stem, line 9) gives three pairs of “relative difference” values, including `3.4e-4`, `3.2e-5`, and `2.2e-3`. “Complete complex outputs” identifies the kind of response but not the normalization: `|H_P-H_N|/|H_P|`, division by the other branch, a symmetric relative error, and `|G_DC|` are different quantities. The three-stage component tuples and the specified capacitive termination are also not identified locally or by a table/case reference, so the numerical examples cannot be located from the paper alone. L3/DR-05/DR-06 require a baseline and conditions. Define the metric once and add an unambiguous case/parameter pointer. These are simulation-reporting omissions, not a demand for additional simulations.

3. **C — The RMS percentages need the observable and norm.** `sections/03a-dynamic-ratio-and-transient-leakage.tex:91`, sentence 3 (ZH line 93) says the signed first-order prediction has `5.59%` and `8.63%` relative RMS discrepancies “against the exact response.” The prediction in Eq. (mismatch-dynamic) is the resistance **error**, whereas the exact model also supplies the resistance **readout**. Dividing RMS disagreement by RMS exact error and by RMS exact readout yields radically different percentages. State the formula and evaluation window for this metric, and explicitly map each percentage to N=3/N=4. L3/DR-05/DR-06; the claim-bearing normalization is missing, so this is C under ER-L01 rather than a surface polish.

4. **C — The delay-based design recommendation needs a frequency qualification.** `sections/05-discussion.tex:15`, sentence 2 (ZH same line): “Reducing the group delay reduces the first-moment tracking bound.” The preceding theory defines `tau` from the derivative at zero frequency (`03a...:24–34`), while group delay generally varies with frequency. A decrease at an unspecified frequency does not identify a decrease of that first moment. Say “zero-frequency group delay”/“the first moment tau,” and retain the positive-kernel and excitation bounds already established. This is a missing claim-bearing condition under L3/L4/ER-L01, not an objection to using the term group delay.

5. **B — Define the coefficient-subscript convention before using the delay formula.** `sections/03a-dynamic-ratio-and-transient-leakage.tex:49` (ZH line 49) introduces `A_{j,1}` and `B_{j,1}` without explicitly defining them. Earlier `A_j,B_j` indexed a stage count, but here `j` indexes a channel. One local definition—“j in {P,N}; A_{j,1}, B_{j,1} are the coefficients of x”—would resolve it while leaving the calculation intact. L3/L5, single notation edit. Also define `R_s` as the fixed known reference resistance at first occurrence (`01-introduction.tex:7`); the intended meaning is recoverable, so I do not call the whole application model defective.

6. **C — Decide the finished paper's evidence scope and rewrite the experimental placeholder accordingly.** `sections/05-discussion.tex:19–27` (ZH same lines) presents a subsection explicitly entitled “Experimental validation to complete the manuscript,” an author-facing “Pending experimental dataset” box, and two future protocol paragraphs. These are transparent, so they are not unsupported experimental claims or D defects. They are still unfinished-manuscript material under L7/§2 Discussion. If this is a theory-and-simulation paper, replace this passage with a finished limitations statement and bounded experimental implications; if bench validation is part of the intended contribution, obtain and report it before submission. The rubric does not itself require every theoretical paper to have hardware experiments.

## Points checked that do not justify manufacturing defects

- The introduction supplies topology, terminal-loading motivation, the ratio-readout problem, and an explicit objective. A blanket “no gap/no objective” verdict would be wrong; the specific weakness is the unfinished antecedent comparison above.
- The abstract has approximately 205 whitespace-delimited words, covers the model, matching result, dynamic result and numerical checks, and does not claim physical experiments. Its length is reasonable, but it starts directly at “We derived” and provides no explicit significance or unresolved-problem move. Under the rubric's five-move Abstract requirement, this is C at paragraph scale even though its individual factual sentences are admissible. One compact motivating/gap sentence, paid for by shortening detailed results, would fix the missing function without padding. ZH main-zh.tex:35 has the same omission. The conclusion has two substantive paragraphs (~137 whitespace-delimited words) and does not need padding. The detailed pulse number in the abstract could be shortened editorially, but that is optional, not a grade defect.
- The core model-to-application connection is stated: buffering turns changing resistance into source-amplitude variation while keeping the ladder LTI (`02...:6`; `03a...:4`). This is a meaningful validity bound, not a hidden moving-resistor filter model. A sensor-system diagram or tolerance-budget example could help application readers but is optional for the stated theoretical scope.
- The paper distinguishes raw common-mode cancellation from DC-calibrated normalized matching (`03a...:59–64`; `05...:13`). It does not silently transfer the raw matching dimensions to all calibrated ratio measurements.
- Figure 10's caption explicitly specifies a 10 ns finite ramp; the text's unit-step peak is identified as a calculated unit-step quantity. That is a stated distinction, not by itself an inconsistent claim. The `9.197601 mV/V` in the abstract agrees dimensionally with `0.009197601` per unit input in the body.
- The second-order worked examples repeat portions of the general proof, but they provide short recoverable tuples P/Q/T and the plotted constructions. Moving some algebra to an appendix may shorten the paper; this is an editorial option, not a mandatory C.
- EN/ZH have the same principal assumptions, theorems, dimensional claims, numerical results and evidence scope. Minor asymmetries: ZH `03a...:24` adds an explanatory sentence about alternating residues that EN omits; ZH `03a...:22` says changing alpha “usually” changes the readout whereas EN explicitly refers to beta>0 examples. Restore the beta>0 qualifier in ZH if literal one-to-one scope is desired, but the surrounding changing-resistance discussion already supplies the context; I do not grade this as a false statement. The shared undefined subscripts, unqualified group-delay recommendation and unspecified metrics are the substantive bilingual issues.
