\section{Conclusions}

Using classical ladder extraction as a coefficient parameterization, we classified termination constraints for grounded multistage differential resistor-capacitor ladders. For a fixed $N$-stage reference branch, raw open-circuit matching left $N$ local degrees of freedom and raw matching at one specified positive resistive termination left $N-1$. Direct-current-normalized matching at one such load left $N$ degrees of freedom. Two distinct known positive resistive loads identified the components under either convention when complete exact responses were available. A polynomial kernel characterized raw matching under rational loads. The ideal-element AC consistency checks covered one to twelve stages.

For calibrated ratio measurements, matched normalized transfers rejected excitation variation exactly for constant resistance. Varying resistance produced an excitation-weighted history average. The signed first-order error combined resistance tracking with $-R(\dot I/I)(\tau_P-\tau_N)$, and three- and four-stage transient cases verified the resulting distinctions. The load-shift formula also predicted the observed common-mode step pulse. The network conditions specify nominal cancellation, while the convolution model specifies the dynamic quantity preserved by that design.
