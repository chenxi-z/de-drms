\section{Dynamic ratio error and transient common-mode leakage}
\label{sec:dynamic}

The buffered front end of Section~\ref{sec:model-plane} supplies $v_P=IR$ and $v_N=IR_s$ to independent positive RC ladders with constant nonnegative resistive loads. Resistance changes the source amplitude; each ladder remains stable and linear time-invariant. Convolutions below include the complete input prehistory, or an equivalent compatible initial state, and require a nonzero reference output. Write $H_j=1/D_j$, $G_j=H_j/H_j(0)=D_j(0)/D_j$, and let $g_j$ be the impulse response of $G_j$. With measured output voltages $u_P,u_N$, direct-current gain calibration gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ratio-readout}
\hat R=R_s\frac{H_N(0)}{H_P(0)}\frac{u_P}{u_N}
=\frac{(g_P\ast IR)(t)}{(g_N\ast I)(t)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Matched channels: an exact weighted-average identity}
\label{sec:dynamic-matched}

For $G_P=G_N=G$, subtracting the instantaneous resistance yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-average}
\hat R(t)-R(t)=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty g(u)I(t-u)[R(t-u)-R(t)]\,\mathrm du}
{(g\ast I)(t)}.
\end{equation}
For positive excitation, the readout is a weighted history average. The readout equals $R_0$ for constant resistance $R_0$ under every excitation waveform with nonzero denominator. When resistance varies, excitation remains in the weights. For example, $I=I_0e^{\alpha t}$ and $R=R_0e^{\beta t}$, with $\alpha,\beta\ge0$ and complete exponential prehistories, give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-exp}
\hat R(t)=R(t)\frac{G(\alpha+\beta)}{G(\alpha)}.
\end{equation}
The examples with $\beta>0$ give different readouts at different $\alpha$ values even for identical channels (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamic-weighting}).

The nodal state matrix of a positive grounded RC ladder has nonnegative off-diagonal entries; the input and output vectors are nonnegative. The matrix exponential gives $g(t)\ge0$. The first absolute moment is therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-moment}
M_1=\int_0^\infty u|g(u)|\,\mathrm du
=\int_0^\infty ug(u)\,\mathrm du=\tau,
\qquad \tau=T\frac{D_x'(0)}{D(0)},\quad T=R_*C_*.
\end{equation}
If $|\dot R|\le L_R$ and $|I|\le I_{\max}$ over the relevant prehistory,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lag-bound}
|\hat R-R|\le\frac{L_R I_{\max}\tau}{|(g\ast I)(t)|}.
\end{equation}
With $0<I_{\min}\le I\le I_{\max}$ throughout that prehistory, the bound becomes $L_R(I_{\max}/I_{\min})\tau$. Finite startup instead requires the actual denominator.

\subsection{Mismatched channels: the current-slew contribution}
\label{sec:dynamic-mismatch}

For different normalized kernels the exact decomposition is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-decomposition}
\hat R-R=\frac{\displaystyle\int_0^\infty g_P(u)I(t-u)[R(t-u)-R(t)]\,\mathrm du}{(g_N\ast I)(t)}
+R\frac{((g_P-g_N)\ast I)(t)}{(g_N\ast I)(t)}.
\end{equation}
Both terms use the reference-channel denominator. For slowly varying $I,R$ and a denominator bounded away from zero, expansion of each convolution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mismatch-dynamic}
\hat R-R\simeq-\tau_P\dot R-R\frac{\dot I}{I}\Delta\tau,
\qquad \Delta\tau=\tau_P-\tau_N.
\end{equation}
Here $A_{j,1}$ and $B_{j,1}$ denote the coefficients of $x$ for channel $j$, and $\tau_j=T(A_{j,1}+gB_{j,1})/(1+gB_j(0))$. Writing $D_j(x)=\sum_k d_{j,k}x^k$, the second moment is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-moment}
\mu_{2,j}=\int_0^\infty u^2g_j(u)\,\mathrm du
=2T^2\left[\left(\frac{d_{j,1}}{d_{j,0}}\right)^2-\frac{d_{j,2}}{d_{j,0}}\right].
\end{equation}
Keeping terms through second order in the time derivatives gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second-order-ratio}
\begin{aligned}
\hat R-R\simeq{}&-\tau_P\dot R-Rh\Delta\tau+\tfrac12\mu_{2,P}\ddot R
+(\mu_{2,P}-\tau_P\tau_N)h\dot R\\
&+R\left[\tfrac12\Delta\mu_2\frac{\ddot I}{I}-\tau_N\Delta\tau h^2\right],
\qquad h=\dot I/I,\quad\Delta\mu_2=\mu_{2,P}-\mu_{2,N}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The terms proportional to $\tau_N$ arise from expanding the reference denominator. The expansion order concerns temporal variation, independently of the ladder order $N$ or the component perturbation size. Equal first moments need not give equal kernels. At $g=0.1$, $(1,1,1,1)$ and $(2,1,3/2,7/360)$ both have $\tau/T=31/12$ but differ in $\mu_2$ by $41T^2/26$ in magnitude. For a twice differentiable input $f$, the first-moment remainder satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-remainder}
|(g_j\ast f)-f+\tau_j\dot f|
\le\tfrac12\mu_{2,j}\sup_{v\le t}|f''(v)|.
\end{equation}
Apply Eq.~\eqref{eq:dynamic-remainder} to $f=IR$ and $f=I$ before expanding the quotient. Fast edges are evaluated with the exact expression. A slower reference channel has $\Delta\tau<0$ and produces positive error during a rising current at constant resistance (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamic-slew}).

For unit-component ladders, the finite-load delay is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-delay}
\frac{\tau(g)}T=\frac{N(N+1)/2+gN(N^2-1)/6}{1+gN}.
\end{equation}
It gives $\tau(0)/T=N(N+1)/2$ and $\lim_{g\to\infty}\tau(g)/T=(N^2-1)/6$. A $10\%$ increase in the last reference capacitor at $g=0.1$ gives $\Delta\tau/T=-N/(N+10)$. Changing $R_*$ at fixed $R_L$ changes both $T$ and $g$; the finite-load formula retains both effects.

\subsection{Matching criterion for a constant resistance}
\label{sec:constant-resistance-match}

The calibrated readout equals every constant $R_0$ for all admissible current histories if and only if $g_P=g_N$. Sufficiency follows from Eq.~\eqref{eq:ratio-readout}; necessity follows because $(g_P-g_N)\ast I=0$ for every probe current forces equality of the kernels. Small perturbations about a positive constant current suffice as probes. Equivalently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-normalized-match}
G_P=G_N\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\frac{D_P}{D_P(0)}=\frac{D_N}{D_N(0)}.
\end{equation}
Raw equality $D_P=D_N$ additionally requires equal direct-current gains. Only with that requirement do the raw matching-family dimensions of Table~\ref{tab:load-dimensions} apply. For varying resistance, matched kernels imply Eq.~\eqref{eq:weighted-average}, with excitation-dependent weights.

\subsection{Transient common-mode leakage after a load change}
\label{sec:cm-pulse}

Each branch has simple negative real poles. With the source shorted, let $K$ be the positive-definite branch nodal conductance matrix and $C$ the positive diagonal branch capacitance matrix. The state matrix is similar to the negative symmetric matrix $-C^{-1/2}KC^{-1/2}$. The symmetric matrix has nonzero adjacent off-diagonal entries. The resulting irreducible tridiagonal matrix has simple negative eigenvalues. Two branches may share poles. The product $D_PD_N$ can therefore contain repeated factors; the residue formula below requires distinct poles after cancellation.

For branches matched at $g_0$, observed at $g$, and with $\Delta B=B_P-B_N$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-load-shift} gives $F=G_{DC}=-(g-g_0)\Delta B/(D_PD_N)$. Since $F(0)=0$ and the poles are stable, a common-mode step of amplitude $V_c$ produces a decaying differential response. For distinct surviving poles $p_k$ in physical frequency units, define $a_k$ as residues of $F(s)/s$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cm-pulse}
y(t)=V_c\sum_k a_ke^{p_kt},\qquad
\int_0^\infty y(t)^2\,\mathrm dt
=V_c^2\sum_{k,j}\frac{a_ka_j}{-(p_k+p_j)}.
\end{equation}
If $r_k$ are instead residues of $F$, then $a_k=r_k/p_k$. Impulse-response energy uses $r_kr_j$ in the sum and is a different quantity. The peak satisfies $\sup|y|\le |V_c|\|h_{DC}\|_1$, where $h_{DC}=\mathcal L^{-1}F$. Repeated poles require the corresponding polynomial-exponential terms.

For P/T in Table~\ref{tab:branches}, matched at $g_0=0.1$ and observed at $g=0.2$, the P-minus-T transfer is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-pt}
F(x)=\frac{0.1x}{(x^2+3.2x+1.4)(x^2+3.3x+1.4)}.
\end{equation}
The dimensionless P/T poles are $-2.8,-2.677033,-0.522967,-0.5$. With $T=10~\mu\mathrm s$, the unit-step peak is $0.009197601$ at $28.20333~\mu\mathrm s$ (Figure~\ref{fig:dynamic-pulse}); the pole closest to the origin sets a $20~\mu\mathrm s$ asymptotic time constant. The step-response squared integral is $3.17497\times10^{-9}V_c^2~\mathrm s$; the response falls below $1\%$ and $0.1\%$ of the peak for all subsequent times after approximately $158.4$ and $209.2~\mu\mathrm s$, measured from the ideal edge. The plotted $10$~ns ramp uses a finite edge, whereas these values refer to the ideal step.




\subsection{Transient verification}
\label{sec:dynamic-verif}

Six baseline native transient cases and two time-step refinements tested three- and four-stage unit-component ladders with $R_*=1~\mathrm{k\Omega}$, $C_*=10~\mathrm{nF}$ and $10~\mathrm{k\Omega}$ terminal loads. The exponential cases were evaluated over $2$--$3$~ms with a maximum time step of $0.1~\mu\mathrm s$; the sinusoidal cases used $20$--$40$~ms and $1~\mu\mathrm s$. The refined maximum steps were $0.05~\mu\mathrm s$ and $0.5~\mu\mathrm s$, respectively. Four matched-channel cases used $R=10~\mathrm{k\Omega}\,e^{200t}$ and either constant current or current proportional to $e^{1000t}$. At $2.5~\mathrm{ms}$, changing excitation changed the matched readout by $5.11377~\Omega$ for $N=3$ and $11.94461~\Omega$ for $N=4$, consistent with Eq.~\eqref{eq:dynamic-exp}. For constant current, the signed tracking errors were $-161.01$ and $-255.79~\Omega$, respectively. The first-moment bounds $200R(t)\tau$ were $162.34$ and $259.08~\Omega$. The current-history difference and the signed tracking error quantify different effects.

Two constant-resistance cases used $R=100~\mathrm{k\Omega}$, $I=8~\mu\mathrm A[1+0.2\sin(2\pi100t)]$, and a $10\%$ increase in the last reference-channel capacitor. They gave $\Delta\tau=-2.30769~\mu\mathrm s$ and $-2.85714~\mu\mathrm s$. Over the $20$--$40$~ms post-warmup window, the RMS of the first-moment prediction residual divided by the RMS of the exact error was $5.59\%$ and $8.63\%$. Evaluating the complete second-order quotient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:second-order-ratio} against the exact steady sinusoidal responses reduced those ratios to $0.224\%$ and $0.532\%$, respectively. These model calculations use the same 100~Hz excitation and component values as the native cases. Across all cases, the maximum simulated-to-exact ratio discrepancy after warmup was below $1.23\times10^{-6}~\Omega$. The two $N=4$ time-step refinements changed the readout by less than $1.4\times10^{-6}~\Omega$.

\subsection{Fast edges and the physical model boundary}

For a current step from zero to a positive value at constant resistance, the calibrated ratio is the quotient of the two normalized step responses. With the last reference capacitor increased by $10\%$, the initial relative error tends to $+10\%$ for both $N=3$ and $N=4$. High-precision evaluation gives crossings of the $10$~ppm error threshold at $457.26$ and $689.30~\mu\mathrm s$, respectively. Equation~\eqref{eq:mismatch-dynamic} uses derivatives and is inapplicable at the discontinuity; the exact convolution or step-response quotient applies there. For $N=4$, increasing capacitors C1 through C4 separately by $10\%$ gives $\Delta\tau=-0.929,-1.714,-2.357,-2.857~\mu\mathrm s$, demonstrating the dependence on component position.

The ratio model places ideal independent buffers before the ladders and excludes output coupling. A finite buffer response multiplies the corresponding channel transfer. For cascaded normalized responses with finite moments, delays satisfy $\tau_{\rm total,j}=\tau_{\rm buffer,j}+\tau_{\rm ladder,j}$. The buffer and ladder delay mismatches can add or cancel. Ladder mismatch alone therefore does not bound the total mismatch from below. A known resistive buffer output impedance can be included in the first ladder resistance. The sensor resistance remains in the source amplitude; a sensor embedded within the ladder changes the ladder state matrix and requires a different time-varying model. Sampling and probe loading enter at the corresponding measurement planes.
