\section{Simulation results and output coupling}

\subsection{General-order verification and model consistency}

The 84-case matrix covered open and resistively loaded branches, several pairing rules, nonuniform same-transfer realizations, fixed-load matching families, reactive loads, source impedance, output coupling and two-load recovery, with orders from one to twelve (Table~\ref{tab:case-matrix}). With the documented 1~m$\Omega$ inductor series resistance included in the independent model, the largest absolute complex node-voltage discrepancy was $1.68\times10^{-14}$~V.

Against the originally intended ideal-inductor reference, two cases exceeded the unchanged $10^{-7}$~V gate, with errors of $4.54\times10^{-7}$ and $3.57\times10^{-7}$~V. Fresh runs specifying $R_{\rm ser}=0$ agreed with the ideal reference to less than $1.8\times10^{-15}$~V; explicit 1~m$\Omega$ runs reproduced the default-model behavior. The discrepancy arose from different element models. Both original failures and the diagnostic records were retained.

At $10$~kHz, the three-stage same-$A$/different-$B$ construction agreed in the open circuit to $|V_P-V_Q|/|V_P|=1.4\times10^{-14}$ and separated under a 10~k$\Omega$ load by $3.4\times10^{-4}$, where the normalization is the reference-branch output magnitude. A fixed-load family agreed at $g=0.1$ to $6.7\times10^{-14}$ and differed at $g=0.2$ by $3.2\times10^{-5}$. A capacitive-load matching family agreed under the specified capacitance to $5.3\times10^{-14}$ and differed under resistive loading by $2.2\times10^{-3}$, all on the same normalized scale. The comparisons tested complete complex outputs; the following second-order examples make the same mechanisms visible with shorter component tuples.

\subsection{A bridging capacitor acts on both differential-output gains}

We connected a capacitor \(C_d\) between the two output nodes while retaining the ideal sources and the specified shunt loads. Let \(Z'_P\text{ and }Z'_N\) denote the branch output impedances in parallel with the respective shunt loads, and let \(H_P\) and \(H_N\) denote the loaded transfers before \(C_d\) was added. Solving the two output-node equations gave

\begin{equation}\label{eq:12}
\begin{aligned}
G_{DC}&=\frac{H_P-H_N}{D_d},\\
G_{DD}&=\frac{(H_P+H_N)/2}{D_d},\\
D_d&=1+sC_d(Z'_P+Z'_N).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The shared factor \(D_d\) cancelled from \(G_{DD}/G_{DC}\) wherever the ratio was defined. The bridging capacitor changed both differential-output gains by the same factor and preserved \(G_{DD}/G_{DC}\). Equal loaded branch transfers also preserved \(G_{DC}\) = 0 after the capacitor was connected.

Equation~\eqref{eq:12} describes the differential output. For single-frequency source phasors, put $E=H_Pv_P$, $F=H_Nv_N$ and $X=sC_d(Z'_NE+Z'_PF)$. The coupled-node solution gives $u_P=(E+X)/D_d$ and $u_N=(F+X)/D_d$, hence $u_P/u_N=(E+X)/(F+X)$. Relative to $E/F$, the first-order change of the quotient is $X(1/E-1/F)$ when $|X/E|$ and $|X/F|$ are small. The shared differential-output factor therefore does not preserve the single-ended quotient. The instantaneous dynamic ratio in Section~\ref{sec:dynamic} assumes independent paths; adding a bridge requires the full coupled two-input response.

For P/Q with \(R_L=10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}\) and \(C_d=820\,\mathrm{pF}\), the maximum complex-ratio difference was \(5.14\times10^{-12}\) relative to the ratio without the bridging capacitor, evaluated over 145 frequency points. The ratio followed the cancellation of the shared factor in Eq.~\eqref{eq:12}.
