\section{Cancellation conditions for an arbitrary number of stages}
\label{sec:general-order}

\subsection{A recursive representation of the ladder}

Consider a branch with $N\geq1$ stages, each containing a positive series resistance $r_j$ followed by a positive shunt capacitance $q_j$. The component normalization and frequency variable are those in Eq.~\eqref{eq:3}. The two-stage circuit in Figure~\ref{fig:circuit} is one instance of this class. With current directed from the network into the terminal load, the chain matrix of stage $j$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-matrix}
M_j(x)=\begin{bmatrix}1+r_jq_jx&r_j\\q_jx&1\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Let $(A_j,B_j)$ be the first row of $M_1\cdots M_j$. Appending the last stage gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-forward}
B_j=r_jA_{j-1}+B_{j-1},\qquad
A_j=A_{j-1}+xq_jB_j,
\quad A_0=1,\ B_0=0.
\end{equation}
Induction in Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-forward} gives $\deg A_N=N$, $\deg B_N=N-1$ and $A_N(0)=1$. The leading coefficients are positive. Each stage matrix has determinant one, so the product does as well; the first-row polynomials have no nonconstant common factor.

The open-circuit transfer and source-shorted output impedance follow from the terminal equation. A common resistive load on each branch then gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-transfer}
H_0=\frac1{A_N},\qquad
\frac{Z_0}{R_*}=\frac{B_N}{A_N},\qquad
H_g=\frac1{A_N+gB_N}.
\end{equation}
For $N=2$, $A_2=1+ax+bx^2$ and $B_2=c+dx$, recovering Eq.~\eqref{eq:5}. Retaining $B_N$ exposes the termination information absent from the open-circuit voltage transfer.

\subsection{Unique recovery and the positive realization test}

The last-stage values can be recovered from the highest-degree coefficients. Writing $\operatorname{lc}(p)$ for the leading coefficient of polynomial $p$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-inverse}
\begin{aligned}
q_j&=\frac{\operatorname{lc}(A_j)}{\operatorname{lc}(B_j)},&
A_{j-1}&=A_j-xq_jB_j,\\
r_j&=\frac{\operatorname{lc}(B_j)}{\operatorname{lc}(A_{j-1})},&
B_{j-1}&=B_j-r_jA_{j-1}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For a positive ladder, these operations undo Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-forward} exactly. At each step, $A_{j-1}$ has degree $j-1$, and $B_{j-1}$ has degree $j-2$ when $j>1$. The final pair is $(A_0,B_0)=(1,0)$. Each ratio is positive and uniquely specified.

Conversely, start with real polynomials of the stated degrees and $A_N(0)=1$. If every subtraction produces the required degree, every recovered component is positive, and the terminal pair is $(1,0)$, reversing the operations reconstructs the original polynomials from a positive ladder. These conditions therefore give a finite necessary and sufficient realization test for this topology. A failed positivity or degree check rejects the candidate coefficient pair.

The forward map from the $2N$ positive components to the $2N$ coefficients of $A_N$ and $B_N$ is polynomial. Its inverse in Eq.~\eqref{eq:general-inverse} is rational with nonzero denominators at every positive realization. Strict positivity and the nonzero leading coefficients persist in a sufficiently small neighborhood. The maps are smooth inverses there, making the coefficient values valid local coordinates for the component family.

\subsection{The number of matching degrees of freedom}

Hold one reference branch fixed. Open-circuit matching fixes the $N$ nonconstant coefficients of $A_N$, leaving $N$ local degrees of freedom in the positive realization domain. Uniform impedance scaling changes $B_N$ by one scalar factor while preserving $A_N$. For $N\geq2$, that scaling follows one direction within a larger matching family.

At a specified $g>0$, matching fixes all coefficients of $D_g=A_N+gB_N$. The constant coefficient fixes $B_N(0)$ through $D_g(0)=1+gB_N(0)$. The remaining $N$ coefficients fix the combinations of $A_N$ and $B_N$. These $N+1$ independent linear constraints leave $N-1$ local degrees of freedom. When $N=1$, a single positive resistive load already leaves a unique component tuple.

At two distinct positive conductances, put $U_i=1/H_{g_i}$. The two complete transfer functions determine
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-two-load}
B_N=\frac{U_2-U_1}{g_2-g_1},\qquad
A_N=\frac{g_2U_1-g_1U_2}{g_2-g_1}.
\end{equation}
Two branches that agree at both loads consequently have identical $A_N$ and $B_N$. Unique recovery then requires identical corresponding components. This argument applies to every finite $N$ in the stated ladder class. It uses complete complex transfer functions, including absolute gain, at two loads.

\subsection{The load type also determines the matching family}

A known rational load can be written as a dimensionless admittance $g(x)=P(x)/Q(x)$, where $P$ and $Q$ are nonzero coprime real polynomials. Assume $QA_N+PB_N$ is not identically zero, so that the loaded response is defined. Equality of the two branch transfers gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-kernel}
H_g=\frac{Q}{QA_N+PB_N},\qquad
Q\Delta A+P\Delta B=0.
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta$ denotes branch P minus branch N. Coprimality implies $\Delta A=PS$ and $\Delta B=-QS$ for a polynomial $S$. The degree bounds and $\Delta A(0)=0$ restrict $S$ to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rational-dimension}
\begin{aligned}
m&=\min\{N-\deg P,\ N-1-\deg Q\},\\
d_g&=\max\{0,m+1-\epsilon\},\qquad
\epsilon=\begin{cases}1,&P(0)\ne0,\\0,&P(0)=0.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $m<0$, the only allowable polynomial is $S=0$. Otherwise, $S$ has $m+1$ coefficients, and a nonzero $P(0)$ fixes its constant coefficient to zero. Intersecting this linear family with the open positive realization domain preserves the local dimension $d_g$.

Table~\ref{tab:load-dimensions} applies Eq.~\eqref{eq:rational-dimension} to common loads. The open circuit, for which $P$ is identically zero, is listed separately. A pure capacitor changes the terminal capacitance and retains $N$ degrees of freedom, whereas a resistor also constrains the zero-frequency gain and reduces the count to $N-1$.

Two distinct known rational admittances also determine the component tuple. Subtracting $\Delta A+g_1\Delta B=0$ and $\Delta A+g_2\Delta B=0$ gives $(g_1-g_2)\Delta B=0$. Since the load functions are not identical, $\Delta B=0$ and then $\Delta A=0$. Possible cancellations in the representation of $H_g$ do not change this conclusion: multiplication by the same known, nonzero $Q$ preserves equality of the branch denominators.

\subsection{Load variation and recovery conditioning}

For independent branches, let $D_i(g)=A_i+gB_i$. The exact common-mode-to-differential gain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-leakage}
G_{DC}=-\frac{\Delta A+g\Delta B}{D_P(g)D_N(g)}.
\end{equation}
If the branches match at $g_0>0$, then $\Delta A=-g_0\Delta B$ and $\Delta B(0)=0$. Under a different common resistive load,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-load-shift}
G_{DC}(g)=-\frac{(g-g_0)\Delta B}{D_P(g)D_N(g)}.
\end{equation}
The zero-frequency leakage remains zero, while the remaining polynomial $\Delta B$ controls the frequency-dependent mismatch. The second-order load-shift formula is the case $\Delta B=x\Delta d$.

Exact identifiability and measurement conditioning require separate checks. Equation~\eqref{eq:general-two-load} divides by the load separation, and a small response perturbation gives $\delta(1/H)\simeq-\delta H/H^2$. Closely spaced loads and strongly attenuated responses amplify measurement errors. Experimental recovery therefore needs a stated frequency band and uncertainty model in addition to the algebraic uniqueness result.
