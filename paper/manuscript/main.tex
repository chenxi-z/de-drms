\begin{abstract}
Terminal loading and direct-current calibration impose different matching constraints on a differential filter. We classified the constraints for positive grounded resistor-capacitor ladders with any finite number of stages and derived the consequences for dynamic ratio measurements. Classical ladder extraction supplied coefficient coordinates for positive realizations. For a fixed reference branch with $N$ stages, raw open-circuit matching left $N$ local degrees of freedom, and raw matching at one specified positive resistive termination left $N-1$. Direct-current-normalized matching at one such load left $N$ degrees of freedom. Two distinct known positive resistive loads identified the components under either convention using complete exact responses. A polynomial kernel extended the raw matching count to rational loads. For constant resistance, equal normalized channel responses rejected excitation changes exactly in the buffered ratio model. Changing resistance produced a current-weighted history average, with a moment expansion separating tracking delay from channel mismatch. An 84-case AC matrix spanning one to twelve stages was checked against independent nodal solutions of the same ideal element models. Three- and four-stage transient cases checked the history dependence. The second-order ratio approximation reduced relative prediction residuals at 100~Hz from $5.59\%$ and $8.63\%$ to $0.224\%$ and $0.532\%$. A load-shifted second-order pair produced a calculated common-mode step peak of $9.198$~mV/V. The analysis distinguishes raw cancellation from calibrated ratio fidelity and makes the terminal constraints explicit.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} differential RC filter; common-mode rejection; resistive termination; output impedance; component recovery.
